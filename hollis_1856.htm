FIELD BOOK OF E. W. HOLLIS
Examination of the Lott Survey and Cypress Bend Road
Tolliver County, Texas — October 1856

October 16

Commenced at the south line of the Nathaniel Lott survey, Patent No. 873, Vol. 19, at the stake described in the former field notes.

Found the post oak standing, though the blaze is much grown over. The pine mentioned in the original description could not be positively identified.

Chain carried by J. P. Vance and Robert Hensley.

Ran East upon the old line. Ground rises gently from the pine woods before falling toward the creek bottom. Timber chiefly pine and post oak, with sweet gum and water oak in the lower ground.

At 640 varas crossed an old wagon trace bearing generally northward. At 1,108 varas entered wet ground. Continued to the eastern corner as described, 1,344 varas.

Corner stake not found. Set temporary stake for the purpose of measurement.

October 17

Returned to the wagon trace to examine its course toward the creek.

At 216 varas crossed a small branch running southeast. At 391 varas encountered a fallen pine obstructing the trace. At 604 varas reached the upper bank of Cypress Bend Creek.

The crossing is used but little at present. Wheel marks visible in the sandy approach. The southern bank is lower and shows evidence of recent washing.

Made observations of the compass in the afternoon. Needle slow to settle at the lower bank, though the instrument answered properly upon returning to higher ground.

Vance supposed iron-bearing stone in the creek bed. No such stone observed.

October 18

Clouds cleared after sunset.

Returned to the open ground above the crossing to examine the variation of the compass.

The needle moved slowly and would not hold its former bearing. While waiting for it to settle, I observed that the stars had disappeared from the sky, excepting one bright light above the eastern timber. I called Vance to examine the sky. He reported seeing the same single light. Within several minutes the other stars were again apparent. The compass returned to its ordinary position shortly afterward.

No satisfactory account of this change was obtained.

October 19

Returned to the southern line.

The old wagon trace appears to have been used for some years, but I found no evidence sufficient to determine whether it was established before or after the Lott survey.

The creek has altered its lower bank in places. The original corner marks in the bottom are uncertain.

Measurements entered in the accompanying field sheet.

No final opinion concerning the road is offered.

E. W. Hollis
Surveyor

Archival note

The notebook was preserved among the papers of the Hollis family and subsequently acquired by the East Texas Land & Water Archive.

The surviving transcription contains four dated entries from a working field book attributed to E. W. Hollis. The accompanying field sheet referred to on October 19 has not been located among the materials reproduced here.

The observations concerning the old wagon trace and the condition of the creek banks do not establish the ownership or legal status of the road.

End of transcription.
