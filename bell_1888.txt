STATE OF TEXAS
COUNTY OF TOLLIVER

PAPERS CONCERNING THE BURNING OF THE PREMISES OF HENRY BELL, AT NEW PROVIDENCE

Office of J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3
November 6–7, A.D. 1888

ARCHIVAL DESCRIPTION

Collection: Tolliver County Justice Papers, 1888–1889
Reference: TC-JP3-1888-41
Description: Four sworn witness statements and one memorandum concerning the destruction by fire of the dwelling and mercantile establishment occupied by Henry Bell at New Providence, near Mercy Crossing.

The papers reproduced below were transcribed from a collection of loose documents attributed to the office of J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3, Tolliver County. They do not constitute the record of a formal examining trial. No indictment, judgment, or final disposition has been located among the surviving materials.

The statements appear to have been taken separately on November 6 and 7, 1888. Original signatures and attestations are reproduced in transcription where legible. Several leaves exhibit damage along their lower margins.



I. STATEMENT OF SARAH FREEMAN

November 6, 1888

Statement made upon oath before J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3.

Sarah Freeman, being duly sworn, stated in answer to questions put to her:

Question. State your name and place of residence.

Answer. Sarah Freeman. I reside at New Providence, near the Cypress Bend road.

Q. How long have you lived there?

A. Since the settlement was first made. Seventeen years, near enough.

Q. Were you acquainted with Henry Bell and his wife?

A. Yes, sir. I have known Henry and Rebecca many years.

Q. Where were you on the evening of October thirteenth last?

A. At my house. My daughter was with me.

Q. Did you observe a fire that evening?

A. I saw a light through the trees. I first took it for a lantern upon the road. Then I saw smoke rising above the store.

Q. Did you go to the place?

A. I went as far as the turn in the road.

Q. What prevented you from going farther?

A. There were men gathered near the Bell gate.

Q. How many men?

A. I counted five. I cannot say whether there were more.

Q. Did you know any of them?

A. I knew two.

Q. Give their names.

A. William Lott and Thomas Rusk.

Q. Are you certain of those persons?

A. I have known Mr. Lott by sight for years. Rusk I knew also.

Q. Did you see either man set fire to the building?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you see any person carrying fire to the building?

A. I did not.

Q. Did you hear anything said?

A. I heard men calling for Henry to come out. I heard one say something had been taken from the crossing.

Q. Did you know what was meant?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you hear Henry Bell answer?

A. I heard a voice from the direction of the house. I believed it was Henry, but I could not distinguish the words.

Q. Did you see him?

A. Not that evening.

Q. When did you last see Henry Bell?

A. On the twelfth, at his store.

Q. And Rebecca Bell?

A. On the afternoon of the thirteenth. She sold me a measure of meal.

Q. Have you seen either of them since?

A. No, sir.

Q. Have you made inquiries concerning them?

A. Yes, sir. We have asked along the roads and at the neighboring settlements.

Q. What information have you obtained?

A. None that I can depend upon.

Q. Had there been any difficulty between Bell and the Lott family?

A. There had been words concerning the road to the crossing.

Q. What was the nature of that difficulty?

A. Henry said the road passed upon his land. I do not know the measurements.

Q. Had you heard any other matter discussed concerning the crossing?

A. People had been speaking of Mr. Edmund Lott's work there.

Q. What was said?

A. That he had instruments set up near the creek, and that somebody had disturbed them.

Q. Was Henry Bell named in connection with that matter?

A. I heard his name mentioned.

Q. By whom?

A. By persons passing through the settlement. I could not name the first who said it.

Q. Had you heard talk of witchcraft or other unusual occurrences?

A. There was talk of many things.

Q. Did you believe such talk?

A. I had no reason to believe it.

Q. Have you any further statement to make?

A. Henry kept a good store. Rebecca worked there beside him. Their house and their goods were burned, and neither of them has been seen by us since.

The foregoing statement was read to the witness, who acknowledged the same to be true according to her knowledge and belief.

SARAH FREEMAN

Witness

Subscribed and sworn before me, this 6th day of November, A.D. 1888.

J. M. HARWOOD

Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3



II. STATEMENT OF JONATHAN BELLAMY

November 6, 1888

Statement made upon oath before J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3.

Jonathan Bellamy, being duly sworn, stated in answer to questions put to him:

Question. State your occupation.

Answer. I am employed by Mr. Edmund Lott in work upon his property.

Q. What work do you perform?

A. I carry instruments, assist in making measurements, and keep the apparatus in order.

Q. Were you at Mercy Crossing on October eleventh?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. With Edmund Lott?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. Did anything unusual occur?

A. One of the instruments did not behave as expected.

Q. Explain what you mean.

A. A needle moved when Mr. Lott said it ought to remain still.

Q. Was the instrument damaged?

A. I could see no damage.

Q. Did Mr. Lott believe anyone had interfered with it?

A. He spoke of the ground having been disturbed.

Q. Did he accuse Henry Bell?

A. Not in my hearing.

Q. Had you seen Bell near the place?

A. I had seen his wagon upon the road at other times.

Q. Was that unusual?

A. No, sir. His mill was near there.

Q. Did you observe anything unusual in the sky?

A. I was attending to the instruments.

Q. Did Mr. Lott observe the sky?

A. He looked upward for some time.

Q. Did he say what he saw?

A. Not that I understood.

Q. Where were you on the evening of October thirteenth?

A. At my lodging near Tolliver Springs.

Q. Who can attest to that?

A. Mrs. Pritchard, who keeps the house.

Q. When did you learn of the fire at Bell's premises?

A. On the following morning.

Q. Did you speak with Edmund Lott concerning it?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. What did he say?

A. He said there had been trouble at New Providence.

Q. Did he say what had become of Henry or Rebecca Bell?

A. No, sir.

Q. Have you any knowledge of their whereabouts?

A. None.

The foregoing statement was read to the witness, who acknowledged the same to be true according to his knowledge and belief.

JONATHAN BELLAMY

Witness

Subscribed and sworn before me, this 6th day of November, A.D. 1888.

J. M. HARWOOD

Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3



III. STATEMENT OF THOMAS RUSK

November 7, 1888

Statement made upon oath before J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3.

Thomas Rusk, being duly sworn, stated in answer to questions put to him:

Question. Were you upon the road leading to the Bell premises on the evening of October thirteenth?

Answer. Yes, sir.

Q. Were you in company with William Lott?

A. I saw Mr. Lott that evening.

Q. Did you go with him to the Bell property?

A. We went to speak with Bell concerning certain property.

Q. What property?

A. Things believed to have been removed from the crossing.

Q. What things?

A. Instruments belonging to Mr. Edmund Lott.

Q. Had you seen those instruments in Bell's possession?

A. No, sir.

Q. Had anyone informed you that Bell possessed them?

A. We had been told that he knew something of the matter.

Q. Who told you?

A. I do not recall who first spoke of it.

Q. How many men accompanied you?

A. There were several upon the road.

Q. Were you armed?

A. I carried my pistol.

Q. Were the others armed?

A. I cannot say what each man carried.

Q. Did you enter Bell's house or store?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you call upon Bell to come outside?

A. We called for him.

Q. Did he answer?

A. I heard somebody speak from the house.

Q. Did you recognize the voice?

A. I believed it was Bell.

Q. Did you threaten him?

A. We wished to speak with him. There was no purpose to do him harm.

Q. Did you observe the fire?

A. I saw flames as we were leaving.

Q. Where did the flames first appear?

A. I could not say. They were already showing above the roof when I noticed them.

Q. Did you attempt to extinguish the fire?

A. It had taken hold before we knew of it.

Q. Did you see Henry Bell leave the premises?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you see Rebecca Bell?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you make any inquiry whether persons remained within?

A. I understood the place to be empty.

Q. From whom did you receive that understanding?

A. I cannot now say.

Q. Was there a dispute concerning the road to Mercy Crossing?

A. Bell had objected to its use.

Q. Upon what grounds?

A. He claimed a portion of it was upon his land.

Q. Was that claim correct?

A. I am not a surveyor.

Q. Was the matter of the road connected with your visit on the thirteenth?

A. The matters had been spoken of together.

Q. In what manner?

A. Bell's objections had made difficulty for persons working near the crossing.

The foregoing statement was read to the witness, who acknowledged the same to be true according to his knowledge and belief.

THOMAS RUSK

Witness

Subscribed and sworn before me, this 7th day of November, A.D. 1888.

J. M. HARWOOD

Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3



IV. STATEMENT OF H. A. MERCER

November 7, 1888

Statement made upon oath before J. M. Harwood, Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3.

H. A. Mercer, County Surveyor, being duly sworn, stated in answer to questions put to him:

Question. Have you recently examined lands adjoining Cypress Bend Creek?

Answer. I have examined the eastern boundary of the Lott tract and the road leading to Mercy Crossing.

Q. Why was this examination made?

A. Henry Bell had objected to the location of the road. I was instructed to compare the existing marks with the older county measurements.

Q. Did you find the boundaries correctly represented?

A. Not in every particular.

Q. Explain.

A. The distance between an established landmark and the creek differs from that recorded in an earlier survey.

Q. By how much?

A. Approximately thirty-seven feet.

Q. Is such a difference material?

A. It is sufficient to require further examination.

Q. Could the creek have altered its course?

A. It could.

Q. Could the original survey have been in error?

A. That is also possible.

Q. Does your examination establish that Bell owned the road?

A. No, sir.

Q. Does it establish that the Lott family owned it?

A. Not conclusively.

Q. Had you completed your examination before the fire?

A. No, sir.

Q. Have you since completed it?

A. I have not.

Q. Have you any knowledge of the cause of the fire?

A. None beyond what has been reported to me.

Q. Have you any knowledge of the whereabouts of Henry or Rebecca Bell?

A. No, sir.

The foregoing statement was read to the witness, who acknowledged the same to be true according to his knowledge and belief.

H. A. MERCER

County Surveyor and Witness

Subscribed and sworn before me, this 7th day of November, A.D. 1888.

J. M. HARWOOD

Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3

V. MEMORANDUM OF J. M. HARWOOD

November 7, 1888

Upon the statements received concerning the burning of the premises occupied by Henry Bell at New Providence, it appears that the dwelling house and adjoining store were destroyed by fire on the evening of October 13 last.

Statements place William Lott, Thomas Rusk, and other persons at or near the premises shortly before the fire was observed. The visit appears to have concerned certain property alleged to have been removed from Mercy Crossing, together with a disagreement respecting the use of the road thereto.

No person examined states that he or she witnessed the setting of the fire.

The whereabouts of Henry Bell and Rebecca Bell remain unknown to this office. No reliable information has been received establishing whether they departed the neighborhood before or after the burning.

The disagreement respecting the road and adjoining boundaries remains unsettled.

The constable is requested to continue inquiry into the cause of the fire and the whereabouts of the persons named above.

J. M. HARWOOD

Justice of the Peace, Precinct No. 3

Tolliver County, Texas
