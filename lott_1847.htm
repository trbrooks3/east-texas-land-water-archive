STATE OF TEXAS
GENERAL LAND OFFICE

PATENT NO. 873 — VOLUME 19

To all to whom these presents shall come, Greeting:

KNOW YE, That by virtue of Headright Certificate No. 184, Fourth Class, issued by the Board of Land Commissioners for Nacogdoches County to Elias Whitcomb, and subsequently assigned to Nathaniel Lott, there is hereby granted by the State of Texas unto the said Nathaniel Lott, his heirs and assigns forever, a tract of land containing three hundred and twenty acres, situated in Nacogdoches County, upon the waters of Cypress Bend Creek, and described by the field notes returned to the General Land Office as follows, to wit:

Beginning at a stake in the pine woods, from which a post oak bears North thirty degrees East, twelve varas, and a pine bears South forty-five degrees West, eight varas;

Thence North, thirteen hundred and forty-four varas, to a stake, from which a red oak bears East, six varas;

Thence East, thirteen hundred and forty-four varas, to a stake on the western margin of the bottom lands of Cypress Bend Creek;

Thence South, thirteen hundred and forty-four varas, to a stake, from which a sweet gum bears South twenty degrees East, nine varas;

Thence West, thirteen hundred and forty-four varas, to the place of beginning.

Containing three hundred and twenty acres of land, more or less.

TO HAVE AND TO HOLD the above-described premises, together with all and singular the rights and appurtenances thereto in anywise belonging, unto the said Nathaniel Lott, his heirs and assigns forever.

IN TESTIMONY WHEREOF, I, J. Pinckney Henderson, Governor of the State of Texas, have caused the seal of the State to be hereunto affixed, and these presents to be countersigned by the Commissioner of the General Land Office, at the City of Austin, this sixteenth day of September, A.D. eighteen hundred and forty-seven.

J. PINCKNEY HENDERSON
Governor of Texas

THOMAS WILLIAM WARD
Commissioner of the General Land Office

ARCHIVAL NOTE
The tract described in this patent lay within Nacogdoches County at the time of issuance. Following the establishment of Tolliver County in 1852, the property was recorded within the new county's jurisdiction.

The transcription preserves the bearings, distances, and identifying landmarks appearing in the patent description. No attempt has been made to reconcile these measurements with subsequent surveys.
