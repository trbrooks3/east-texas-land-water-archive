The Tolliver Daily.
TOLLIVER COUNTY, TEXAS, FRIDAY, OCTOBER 19, 1888.
W. Stevens, Editor.

RAILROAD MATTERS.

We are informed that further arrangements are being made respecting the proposed railway connection between Nacogdoches and Palestine, of which mention was made in these columns in May last.

There has been considerable interest among our citizens in the undertaking, particularly as it concerns travel and trade with Fort Worth. It appears, however, that the company does not contemplate laying its road through Tolliver County.

Our people must for the present continue to take the cars at Nacogdoches.

THE COTTON MARKET.

Reports from Nacogdoches indicate a fair movement of cotton this week. A number of our farmers have carried their cotton to that place rather than dispose of it here.

The merchants of Palestine are likewise reported to be doing a good business, though some complaint is made of the prices received by the farmers.

METHODISTS AT NEW PROVIDENCE.

Work has commenced upon the Methodist meeting-house at New Providence.

The Rev. John Renfro has conducted a number of successful revival meetings at the grounds near that place during the past five years.

It is expected that the house will be ready for services shortly after the new year.

THE ROADS.

Much complaint is made of the condition of the road leading into Nacogdoches County. Several wagons carrying cotton have had considerable difficulty upon the lower ground.

It is hoped that the commissioners will give the matter their attention before the winter rains set in.

FIRE AT NEW PROVIDENCE.

The dwelling and store of Mr. Henry Bell, near Mercy Crossing, were destroyed by fire on Saturday night last, at about 9 o'clock.

A number of the citizens turned out and water was procured from the creek, but little could be done toward saving either building. Both were almost wholly consumed.

It is reported that some words passed between Bell and four or five white men near the store previous to the fire. We have been unable to learn the particulars.

Neither Bell nor his wife has been heard from since Saturday, and there is much speculation as to their whereabouts. Some believe they have left the county.

No other buildings were destroyed.

The origin of the fire is not known. The officers are making inquiry, and persons having knowledge of the affair or of the whereabouts of the Bells are requested to communicate with the sheriff.

FROM PALESTINE.

There is some talk among the gentlemen of Anderson County of holding an exhibition of livestock and agricultural products next autumn.

We see no reason why the farmers of Tolliver should not undertake something of the same sort. There are several gentlemen in this county whose stock would make a respectable showing anywhere in East Texas.

BARN RAISING.

Mr. A. Hays and his wife invite their neighbors to assist in the raising of a new barn on Sunday, the 21st inst., after services.

THE ELECTION.

The general election will be held Tuesday, November 6.

The polls will be opened at the Baptist church at New Providence and at the county offices for the accommodation of qualified voters in their respective precincts.

The presidential candidates of the two principal parties are Gen. Benjamin Harrison, Republican, and President Grover Cleveland, Democrat.

State and county officers are to be elected on the same day.

A NEW PHYSICIAN.

Dr. J. M. Caldwell, lately of Palestine, has taken rooms near the county offices and will commence the practice of medicine here on Monday next.

The doctor comes well recommended and will attend calls in the country when required.

MATRIMONIAL.

Mr. and Mrs. E. Freeman announce the engagement of their daughter to Mr. Jacob Lott, eldest son of S. and C. Lott.

Friends wishing to send their congratulations may address them to the Freeman residence at New Providence. A gathering of the families and their friends will be held in November.

BORN.

On Tuesday, the 16th inst., to I. Washington and his wife R., twins.

Mother and children reported doing well.

W. Stevens, Editor and Proprietor.
